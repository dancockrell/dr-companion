# The Crossing: Tabletop Scene-Plate Prompt Packet

Status: **first Magnific art-direction batch**

## Production unit

The primary visual reference is a **20–30 metre tabletop scene plate**, not an
isolated object or a perfect tile. A reference plate is a coherent DragonRealms
area on a deep forest-green felt or painted-cloth under-mat: broad
terrain/building masses plus loose scatter props. It is viewed from a readable
three-quarter isometric camera. The MUD room graph remains truth.

**Important boundary:** these are prompts for concept and composition
references. Godot does not turn prompt prose or a generated plate into runtime
geometry. The Godot scene assembler receives the same intent—required room
facts, palette, silhouette, prop families, clear exit mouths, and exclusions—
then places reviewed licensed-store or project-made meshes. A reference can
teach us what to build; it is not itself a build artifact.

The reusable kit remains a vocabulary. Purchased trees, hedges, benches,
barrels, flowers, bridge bits, facades, terrain dressing, and connectors are
the geometry that can be scattered between larger project-made landmark
elements. They do not need to tessellate perfectly. The tabletop conversion
should feel intentionally assembled, not like a failed simulated city.

## Universal Magnific suffix

Append this paragraph to every prompt below:

> Make this as an original premium tabletop-roleplaying-game terrain diorama,
> viewed from a clear three-quarter isometric camera high enough to read the
> whole playable area. It sits on a subtly visible deep forest-green felt or
> painted-cloth under-mat with a thin dark wood or earth-cut edge. Scenery is
> chunky, intentional, and lightly toy-like rather than realistic: broad
> painted-resin color planes, pleasant bevels, clean silhouette masses, and
> deliberate open paths for 28–32 mm miniatures. Build one complete coherent
> area, not isolated objects. No people, creatures, player characters, live
> merchandise, readable lettering, logos, UI, labels, grid, floating
> measurements, photorealism, dense foliage noise, cinematic background, or
> real-world heraldry. Keep the background a simple studio-dark neutral so the
> scene can be separated and reused.

## Shared factual and art rules

- **Era/style:** DragonRealms late-medieval urban fantasy, practical river-trade
  city; never literal London, Renaissance, industrial, historical simulation,
  or grimdark.
- **The Crossing:** maintained central trade city near the Segoltha and
  Oxenwaithe. Use river/canal edges, bridges, wet low margins, markets, civic
  routes, and greens; do not turn the whole city into swamp.
- **Truth:** a visible door, path, bridge, hedge breach, or arch has no game
  meaning without its matching room-graph exit. Never add an invented route.
- **Scale:** larger pieces can be irregular. Keep every intended path mouth
  2m-equivalent and visibly clear. Scatter props cannot block a real exit.
- **Characters/items:** leave environment renders unpopulated. Plastic
  miniatures and live floor items are separate data-driven layers.

## First environment requests

### A. Town Green: north/east gathering ground

Build the north/east portion of The Crossing's Town Green as one 25m square
diorama plate. Its centre is tranquil maintained open greensward of bent grass,
with a narrow warm cobblestone strip along the north edge between grass and a
clipped privet hedge. Beyond the hedge, place a restrained unbranded low
weaponsmith/service frontage in warm plaster and dark timber, one simple closed
service door, and no forge, weapons, stock, vendor, smoke, or signage. Toward
the northeast the Green broadens around one dominant ancient oak: low gnarly
trunk, broad friendly canopy, and a generous clear gathering ring at its base.
Add only two or three low limestone benches and restrained shrubs where they
soften a boundary; keep obvious grass/cobble routes between north, east, and
the central clearing.

This is a calm cared-for civic green inside an old fantasy trading city, not a
park illustration. Make oak, hedge, cobble, lawn, and blank frontage separate
scenic masses that can later be cut/recoloured. Do not include the armory hedge
breach, performance bower, stalls, festival crowd, omnipresent flowers, magic,
labels, statues, or a cathedral.

### B. Town Green: performance bower and armory boundary

Build a 25m square Town Green plate with calm central lawn and two distinct
nearby feature corners. In one corner make a modest open modwyn-vine bower:
four warm wood posts, sparse chunky green vines, a simple pergola, one limestone
bench, and five readable stump stools around a small open gathering area. It is
a quiet informal performance/garden nook with a clear approach, not a stage,
tavern patio, or permanent festival set. Along a separate hedge boundary create
one deliberate hacked hedge breach with two plain wooden planks across a shallow
harmless dip. Behind it place a small unpainted timber armory/workshop exterior
with a blank hook rail and abstract removable equipment-sample shapes—no actual
weapons, forge, banner, proprietor, or readable sign.

Make bower and armory boundary visibly separate room features joined by
maintained lawn and low privet runs, not one overstuffed attraction. Preserve a
clear 2m-equivalent route through the hedge gap and broad sightlines across the
lawn. The armory feels practical/rough; the bower feels gentle/social; neither
claims more than the room descriptions support.

### C. Segoltha river crossing and working quay

Build a 30m-wide Crossing riverside diorama. One side is a broad slow blue-green
river ribbon with thick toy-resin water and two or three large clean highlight
planes, edged by an irregular maintained stone/earth quay. A warm-stone bridge
or covered culvert carries one clear central cobble route from the city side
toward the opposite bank. Add a small blank dock gangplank, two low bollards,
two sealed generic barrels, and generic cargo crates safely off the route. The
city side has low plaster/timber rear massing and one blank arch/door threshold;
the river edge has modest darker-green wet margin, not a marsh.

This is a working medieval-fantasy river-city connection, not a pirate port,
fishing village, Venice, or photoreal harbor. Do not include boats, sailors,
cargo labels, nets, signs, named bridge plaques, guards, warehouse skyline,
random reeds, or real-world flags. Every crossing and dock entrance remains
visually clear for a MUD-authorized transition.

### D. Mongers' Square and market approach

Build a 25m square civic-market plate for Mongers' Square. Use a broad warm
cobble apron with slightly irregular chunky stones and several clear approach
lanes. Centre it on a low neutral round stone fountain basin with a simple blank
central plinth and a clean painted-resin water disk. At the perimeter place two
or three open unbranded market-stall shells with muted canvas canopies and empty
counters, plus a pale-column arcade fragment or modest civic frontage. Use small
scatter dressing—one bench, sealed barrels, blank crate cluster, low planter—to
avoid sterility while keeping circulation open.

It is an adaptable market civic space, not a permanent fair. Do not add vendors,
customers, goods, prices, hanging text, festival banners, coins, guards, live
animals, named shop identity, or crowd clutter. Empty fixtures receive live
content later; the square stays legible when no activity is reported.

## Admission workflow

1. Generate one reference plate per prompt, inspect it at the equivalent
   in-game crop, then change one meaningful variable for a variation.
2. Preserve creation ID, complete prompt, tool/model, intended room/area role,
   and review result in the asset ledger as **reference-only** provenance.
3. Reject beautiful but incorrect references. Required room facts and clear
   graph-exit anchors outrank surface detail.
4. Turn the accepted reference into an assembly brief: required mesh families,
   target silhouette, palette, prop density, clear lanes, and exclusions. Do
   not extract or convert an entire reference plate into runtime geometry.
5. Admit actual store or project-made assets for foliage, props, terrain
   dressing, landmarks, and interiors when they meet the kit's connector,
   licensing, scale, and tabletop-material gates. Record provider, license,
   attribution, scale, palette adaptation, and packaged runtime path in the
   same ledger.
